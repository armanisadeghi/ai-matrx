<artifact type="flashcards" id="87f61730-5ea2-45a9-b082-398499a335d0" version="1" title="Flashcards 1">

---

Front: What is "curdling," and what causes it?
Back: The process where milk proteins (casein) clump into solid curds, triggered by adding rennet and/or acid that destabilizes the proteins.

---

Front: What are the two things milk separates into during cheesemaking?
Back:
- **Curds** — the solids (proteins + fat) that become cheese
- **Whey** — the liquid left behind, rich in water and lactose

---

Front: What does rennet actually do?
Back: An enzyme (chymosin) that snips a specific bond in casein proteins, letting them bond together and set the milk into a gel.

---

Front: Why are some cheeses aged for years?
Back:
- Enzymes & microbes break down fats and proteins over time
- This develops deeper flavor and firmer texture
- Moisture slowly evaporates, concentrating taste

## 🍳 Recipe
A real structured recipe card.

<artifact type="recipe" id="5ebbfed2-a3f6-4743-84c3-82212781c212" version="1" title="Cooking Recipe 11">
### 30-Minute Fresh Mozzarella

**Yields:** 1 lb (about 450g)  
**Time:** 35 minutes (10 min prep + 25 min active)  

---

#### Ingredients:
- 1 gallon whole milk (NOT ultra-pasteurized — this is critical)
- 1 ½ tsp citric acid, dissolved in ¼ cup cool water
- ¼ tsp liquid rennet, diluted in ¼ cup cool unchlorinated water
- 1 tsp salt
- Cold water + ice (for an ice bath)

---

#### Instructions:

1. **Acidify:** Pour the citric acid solution into the cold milk and stir thoroughly. This lowers the pH so the milk can set properly.
2. **Heat:** Warm the milk slowly to 90°F (32°C), stirring gently. Remove from heat.
3. **Add rennet:** Stir in the diluted rennet using an up-and-down motion for about 30 seconds, then stop. Cover and let sit undisturbed for 5 minutes (the curd should look like soft tofu and pull cleanly from the side).
4. **Cut the curd:** Slice the curd into a 1-inch checkerboard. Return to low heat and bring to 105°F (40°C), stirring slowly as the curds separate from the yellowish whey.
5. **Drain & heat:** Scoop curds into a bowl. Microwave 1 minute, drain off whey, then sprinkle with salt.
6. **Stretch:** Reheat in short bursts and fold/stretch the curd until it's glossy and smooth like taffy (it should stretch without breaking). 
7. **Shape & cool:** Form into balls and drop into the ice bath to firm up.

(Tip: if it tears instead of stretches, it needs to be a touch hotter.)

---
And that's it — warm, milky mozzarella made with your own hands in less time than a TV episode. 🧀
